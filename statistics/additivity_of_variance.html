Additivity of Variance & $SS$

Why do Variance and Sum of Squares ($SS$) of $Z = X + Y$ project onto $y = x$?

1. Lines of Constant Sum: Defining $Z = X + Y$, the line $Z = C$ (or $y = -x + C$) has slope $-1$. Every point on a slate contour line shares the exact same sum $Z$.

2. Orthogonal Projection: Moving perpendicular to $y = -x$ along $y = x$ changes $Z$ fastest. Each green point's distance from the mean along $y = x$ is $d_{\perp} = \frac{Z_i - \bar{Z}}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{(X_i+Y_i) - (\bar{X}+\bar{Y})}{\sqrt{2}}$.

3. Sum of Squares ($SS$) Decomposition: Squaring and summing all deviations $\sum(Z_i - \bar{Z})^2$ yields $SS_{X+Y} = SS_X + SS_Y + 2\,SP_{XY}$, where $SP_{XY} = \sum(X_i-\bar{X})(Y_i-\bar{Y})$ is the Sum of Cross-Products.

4. Dividing by $df = N - 1$: Dividing the entire $SS$ identity by $N - 1$ yields the sample variance law: $s_{X+Y}^2 = s_X^2 + s_Y^2 + 2r s_X s_Y$.

Why does positive correlation shrink $SS_{X-Y}$ and $\text{Var}(Z)$ for $Z = X - Y$?

1. Lines of Constant Difference: Defining $Z = X - Y$, the line $Z = C$ (or $y = x - C$) has slope $+1$. Along $y = x$, $X = Y$ so the difference $Z = 0$.

2. Projection & Cancelation: When $\rho > 0$, points cluster along $y = x$. Projecting onto $y = -x$ gives distance $d_{\perp} = \frac{|Z_i - \bar{Z}|}{\sqrt{2}}$, producing tiny deviations because shared fluctuations cancel out.

3. Sum of Squares ($SS$) Decomposition: Subtracting $Y$ flips the sign of the cross-product term: $SS_{X-Y} = \sum(Z_i - \bar{Z})^2 = SS_X + SS_Y - 2\,SP_{XY}$.

4. Geometric Cosine Law: Positive $SP_{XY}$ closes the hinge angle between the $\sqrt{SS_X}$ and $-\sqrt{SS_Y}$ vectors, directly reducing $SS_{X-Y}$.
