6. One chi-square framework

df = k − 1 − m

One engine

One unified test

A test of independence is a goodness-of-fit test whose expected counts are estimated from the sample’s row and column totals, rather than given in advance by an external theory.

$\chi^2 = \sum (O-E)^2/E$
$E_{ij} = (R_i \times C_j)/N$

Degrees of freedom and power

Estimating margins spends df

Estimating the baseline proportions uses m = (r − 1) + (c − 1) extra degrees of freedom, leaving (r − 1)(c − 1) instead of rc − 1. A magnet on one cell is partly absorbed by the margins, so independence has less power when the true probabilities are already known.

$df = (rc - 1) - (r + c - 2)$
$= (r - 1)(c - 1)$

Known two-way blueprint

Two factors with known probabilities

If theory already gives a probability for every cell, including the wheel’s 10–8–8–10 color × parity layout, flatten the table into rc categories and use goodness-of-fit.

$m = 0$
$df = rc - 1 = 3$

A compound null

Testing association with goodness-of-fit

Goodness-of-fit can look for association when both factors’ probabilities are known in advance, using E = N p q. A large χ² can mean the factors are associated, or that the assumed margins were wrong.

Parameters estimated from data

One factor with data-driven counts

A one-way goodness-of-fit test can also estimate expected counts, as when a Poisson or binomial family is fit with an unknown parameter. Subtract one degree of freedom for each estimated parameter m.

$df = k - 1 - m$
A Poisson fit uses m = 1.
