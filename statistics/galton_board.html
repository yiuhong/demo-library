Reading the $k$-axis and $\pm\sigma$ markers

Each bin along the horizontal axis corresponds to $k$ ($0 \le k \le n$), counting how many times a bead bounced right out of peg rows (, ).

The red dashed line marks the theoretical mean $\mu = np$. The purple dashed lines mark one standard deviation ($\mu \pm \sigma$), where the normal curve has its inflection points (enclosing about $68.3\%$ of the distribution). Amber dots are the exact binomial probabilities; the dark blue curve is the normal approximation.

From the binomial count to a normal curve

1. Exact binomial PMF Discrete dots

Each bead passes through $n$ rows of pegs. Let $X_i \in \{0, 1\}$ denote a left ($0$) or right ($1$) bounce at row $i$ with probability $\mathbb{P}(X_i = 1) = p$. The final horizontal bin index $k$ is the sum of $n$ independent Bernoulli trials:

Theoretical binomial probability
Binomial coefficient: $\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}$
2. Gaussian density approximation Continuous curve

Because $X$ is the sum of $n$ independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) variables with mean $p$ and variance $p(1-p)$, its exact mean and variance over $n$ rows are $\mu = np$ and $\sigma^2 = np(1-p)$. For large $n$, $P(X=k)$ converges to the Gaussian density $f(k)$:
