The $\chi^2$ Distribution

The Chi-Square distribution represents the sum of squared independent standard normal variables.

Theoretical Moments

Mean ($k$)
3
Variance ($2k$)
6
Samples drawn: 0

1. Hard boundary at 0: Squaring any number yields $\ge 0$.

2. Right-skew at low $k$: ~68% of $Z \in [-1, 1]$. When squared, these shrink toward 0 (e.g., $0.5^2 = 0.25$), stacking heavily near 0. Rare outliers explode outward.

3. Central Limit Theorem: As $k$ increases, summing more independent variables pulls the shape toward a normal distribution. Because $\chi^2_k$ is exactly the sum of $k$ independent variables (the $Z_i^2$ terms), rare extreme high values are increasingly likely to be balanced out by the much more common values near zero as $k$ grows, guaranteeing their overall sum clusters symmetrically into a bell curve.
