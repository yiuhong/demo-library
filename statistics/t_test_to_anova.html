From T-Test to ANOVA: Partitioning Variation

Relationship between difference and variance

The independent t-test is typically conceptualized as evaluating the mean difference between two groups relative to the Standard Error (SE), asking the fundamental question: "Is there a difference in scores between the groups?" However, it can also be understood by partitioning the Total Variation in all scores into two distinct pieces. This conceptually shifts our question to: "Does knowing group membership tell us about the variation in scores?"

1. Within-Group Variation (Sum of Squares Within / SSW)

Natural spread around their own group's mean (Noise).

2. Between-Group Variation (Sum of Squares Between / SSB)

Spread between the group means and the grand mean (Signal).

The Conceptual Bridge
1. The Anchor Point: A T-Test measures the direct distance from Group A to B. Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) changes the anchor point, measuring how far each group pulls away from the Grand Mean.
2. Accounting for "Mass": A T-Test's signal (MD) ignores sample size. ANOVA multiplies by sample size (n) because it calculates the total shifted "mass" of every individual, not just the abstract averages.
How math maps MD to n

The Idea: The Mean Difference (MD) relates to SSB, which involves the distance from group means to the Grand Mean, weighted by the number of individuals (n). Because our two groups are equal in size, the Grand Mean is perfectly halfway between them—thus bringing a factor of 1/2 into the formula naturally!

Squaring that half-distance (MD²/4) and multiplying by the weight of individuals (n) for both groups gives: n(MD²/4) + n(MD²/4) = (n/2)MD².

Unequal Groups? The Grand Mean acts like a center of gravity. The distance ratio depends directly on the group size ratio! The universal mathematical factor that seamlessly handles this gravity for both Signal and Noise is (n₁ × n₂) / (n₁ + n₂). When n₁ = n₂ = n, this perfectly reduces back to n/2.

The Mathematical Steps from Difference to Variance:
  • 1. Square it: Convert linear units (MD and SE) into squared variances.
  • 2. Scale it: Multiply by the sample size weight (n/2 = 2.5) to find the Mean Squares (MSB & MSW).
  • 3. Un-average it: Multiply by degrees of freedom (df) to get the raw Sum of Squares (SSB & SSW).

Linear vs Squared

The core difference between a T-Test and ANOVA is a simple shift in scale. A T-Test compares linear units (raw mean differences), while ANOVA compares squared units (variances). Because they measure the exact same underlying reality, calculating the ratio of Signal to Noise using either method yields the exact same statistical truth!

  • 1. A Universal Metric: A simple subtraction only works for exactly two groups. Variance solves this by measuring how far every group is from a shared Grand Mean, scaling effortlessly to handle any number of groups where simple subtraction fails.
  • 2. The Same Reality: For exactly two groups, measuring the direct distance between them (T-Test) and measuring their variance from the Grand Mean (ANOVA) are simply two ways of describing the exact same gap.
