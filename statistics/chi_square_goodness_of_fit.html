Reference. Pearson’s criterion is Pearson (1900), On the criterion that a given system of deviations from the probable in the case of a correlated system of variables is such that it can be reasonably supposed to have arisen from random sampling (Philosophical Magazine, Series 5, 50, 157–175). The expected-count check is Cochran (1954), Some methods for strengthening the common χ² tests (Biometrics, 10, 417–451).

How a Pearson term is built

The term $\dfrac{(O - E)^2}{E}$ is squared error in units of the expected variance of a count. How many units of ordinary random luck away from the expectation did this category land?

Numerator: $(O - E)^2$ Squared distance between the observed count and the expected count. Squaring keeps every miss positive and gives large misses extra weight.
Denominator: $E$ Expected variance of a count of that size. A miss of 3 is unsurprising when $E$ is large and surprising when $E$ is small.
Ratio: $(O - E)^2 / E$ One category’s contribution to $\chi^2$. The contributions add up to the test statistic.
