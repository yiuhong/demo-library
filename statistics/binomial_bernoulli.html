Theoretical foundation

Note on the Bernoulli process

A Bernoulli process is a sequence of independent and identically distributed binary random variables \(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n\). Each coin toss is a Bernoulli trial with \(X_i \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p)\):

$$P(X_i = 1) = p \quad \text{(success / heads)}, \qquad P(X_i = 0) = 1 - p = q \quad \text{(failure / tails)}$$

Three conditions have to hold:

  • Binary outcomes. Every trial \(i\) is one of two mutually exclusive outcomes (\(X_i \in \{0, 1\}\)).
  • Constant probability. The chance of heads \(p\) is the same on every trial.
  • Independence. Earlier tosses say nothing about the next one (\(P(X_i = 1 \mid X_1, \dots, X_{i-1}) = p\)).

Connection to the binomial distribution. The number of heads across \(n\) trials is the sum of those indicators, and that sum follows a binomial distribution: $$X = \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i \sim \text{Binomial}(n, p)$$ Each rightward bounce on the Galton board is this same trial. The bin is this same count.

Combinatorics

Note on the combination

Any one sequence with exactly \(k\) heads and \(n - k\) tails has probability \(p^k(1-p)^{n-k}\). Those \(k\) heads can sit in any of the \(n\) positions.

The binomial coefficient (“\(n\) choose \(k\)”) counts the sequences with \(k\) heads. Order within the heads does not matter:

$$\binom{n}{k} = {}_nC_k = C(n, k) = \frac{n!}{k!\,(n - k)!}$$
  • Factorial. \(n! = n \times (n-1) \times \cdots \times 1\), and \(0! = 1\).
  • Symmetry. \(\binom{n}{k} = \binom{n}{n-k}\). Choosing the heads is the same as choosing the tails.
  • Boundaries. \(\binom{n}{0} = \binom{n}{n} = 1\). The probabilities add to 1: \(\sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k} = 1\).
