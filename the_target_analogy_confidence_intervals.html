The Target Analogy

How the "wandering habits" of a sample translate into a Confidence Interval.

Controls: Shooter Skill (Standard Error)

Statistically, a Good Shooter means a small Standard Error (e.g., a large sample size). A Bad Shooter means a large Standard Error (e.g., a small sample size).

Controls: Net Size (Multiplier)

Choose how many Standard Errors (SE) wide to cast your net. A wider net increases confidence but decreases precision.

1. The Sampling Distribution

The God View: We can see the True Population Mean (the Red Bullseye) and 100 Sample Means (the Blue Darts) scattered around it.

The blue circle represents the Standard Error (the mathematical wandering habits of the darts). Notice how the circle originates from the center.

Key takeaway: The true mean casts a predictable net that catches a known percentage of sample means.

2. The Reversal (One Confidence Interval)

The Researcher's View: In the real world, the True Mean is invisible. You only get to throw one single dart.

How do we find the True Mean? We take the known behavior from Step 1 (the blue circle) and anchor it to our one single dart.

Key takeaway: Because distance is symmetrical, drawing the net around the dart works exactly as well as drawing it around the bullseye.

3. The Long Run (Multiple CIs)

The Truth Revealed: Let's look at all 100 samples again, but this time, let's draw a Confidence Interval net around every single one of them.

Nets that successfully trap the Red Bullseye turn Green. Nets that completely miss turn Red.
