An early sensory positive component reflecting initial processing in the primary auditory cortex. It is often used to study sensory gating (e.g., the brain's ability to filter out redundant auditory information).

The Signal-to-Noise Problem

Background EEG activity (alpha, beta, delta, theta waves) typically ranges between $20\,\mu\text{V}$ and $100\,\mu\text{V}$. In contrast, cognitive sensory ERPs are buried at values between $1\,\mu\text{V}$ and $10\,\mu\text{V}$. Individual stimulus trials look identical to random background noise.

The Averaging Formulation

An ongoing recording is divided into stimulus-locked trials $S_j(t) = s(t) + n_j(t)$, where $s(t)$ is the consistent time-locked ERP wave (signal), and $n_j(t)$ is the random uncorrelated noise (background EEG) on trial $j$.

$$\text{Average}(t) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^{N} S_j(t) = s(t) + \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N} n_j(t)$$

Mathematical Noise Suppression

Assuming the background EEG noise $n_j(t)$ is Gaussian with a mean of zero and variance of $\sigma^2$, and is completely uncorrelated across trials, the variance of the averaged noise goes down with $N$ trials:

$$\sigma_{\text{average}} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}}$$

Thus, with 100 trials, background noise is suppressed by a factor of $\sqrt{100} = 10$, bringing a $20\,\mu\text{V}$ noise down to a manageable $2\,\mu\text{V}$, revealing the pure sensory ERP.
